## 3. Product and impact

### What problem are you solving? — max. 500 characters

<!-- FF:problem:500 -->
Many people have concrete political concerns but never contact an elected representative. They do not know which level of government is responsible, whom to address, or how to turn frustration into a credible letter. Time, writing confidence and political knowledge become barriers, especially for people already carrying work, care or financial pressures.
<!-- /FF -->

### What is your product solution? — max. 500 characters

<!-- FF:solution:500 -->
A user describes a concern and enters a German postal code. Brief-nach-Berlin identifies the responsible federal, state or local level, finds the relevant representative or office, and drafts a one-page personal letter. The user reviews, edits, handwrites and sends it. There is no account or automatic dispatch, and concerns and letters are not persistently stored.
<!-- /FF -->

### What is your advantage over alternatives? — max. 500 characters

<!-- FF:advantage:500 -->
Petition platforms collect signatures; public Q&A sites publish exchanges; generic AI can write text but does not know German responsibilities or constituencies. Brief-nach-Berlin combines jurisdiction routing, open political data, private AI drafting and a deliberate human action. It creates personal letters rather than a message blast, keeps the sender in control, and publishes its core as open source.
<!-- /FF -->

### Stage details — max. 500 characters

<!-- FF:stage:500 -->
Publicly launched in Germany in March 2026. By September 4, the service had generated about 1,800 letter drafts. It now routes concerns across federal, state and local levels and has been covered by major German outlets including Süddeutsche Zeitung, Handelsblatt, ZEIT, Stern and public radio. We track generated drafts, not verified delivery, and are adding privacy-preserving send confirmation and outcome measurement.
<!-- /FF -->

### Target users and how you reach them — max. 500 characters

<!-- FF:target_users:500 -->
The service is open to anyone in Germany, with an initial focus on busy adults and people making their first direct political contact. We reach them through trusted intermediaries: adult education, schools, universities, unions, civil-society groups and news media. Small, nonpartisan paid tests will be optimized for completed and self-reported sent letters, not clicks or ideological persuasion.
<!-- /FF -->

### Significant risk, challenge and outcome — max. 1,000 characters

<!-- FF:risk:1000 -->
The easiest version of this idea would have been an AI mass-email tool. I chose a harder constraint: no automatic sending, no stored political profiles, and no removal of the citizen's own decision and effort. That meant solving German jurisdiction and constituency routing across federal, state and local levels while keeping the flow under three minutes. Early versions covered only the Bundestag, so local and state concerns could reach the wrong level. I rebuilt the routing and data layer, expanded coverage, and kept the user responsible for reviewing, personalizing and sending the letter. The result is a live nationwide service that has generated about 1,800 drafts and earned national media attention without a marketing budget. The lesson was that responsible friction can be part of the product: scale should mean more genuine civic actions, not more synthetic messages.
<!-- /FF -->
